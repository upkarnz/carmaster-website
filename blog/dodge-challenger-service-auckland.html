Why it's different

Owning a Challenger in New Zealand is its own thing.

Dodge doesn't officially distribute in New Zealand, so almost every Challenger on the road here arrived as a private import — most commonly the 3.6L Pentastar V6, the 5.7L HEMI V8, the 6.4L 392 HEMI, or one of the supercharged Hellcat and Redeye variants. That means two things for ownership: parts and specialist knowledge aren't sitting at every dealership on the corner, and a workshop that hasn't seen many of these cars is learning on your car, not on someone else's first.

None of that makes a Challenger difficult to own well. It just means the value of a workshop that actually works on American muscle cars — rather than one that will reluctantly take the job — is higher than it would be for a mainstream Japanese or Korean import.

From the workshop

What a real Challenger service looks like

On a recent Challenger service, we ran Motul 0W-40 full-synthetic oil with a Ryco performance oil filter — a spec suited to the higher sustained operating temperatures a HEMI or supercharged engine sees compared with a standard passenger car. During the same multi-point inspection, we found the rear tyres significantly worn, well below a safe tread depth. We flagged it immediately and recommended replacement before the car left the workshop. On a rear-wheel-drive car putting real power down, worn rear tyres are a genuine safety risk, not just a box to tick for WOF — we'd rather tell you what your car actually needs than what's easiest to ignore.

Worth knowing

Modifications and WOF compliance

Because most Challengers in New Zealand are privately imported, modifications that would be a non-issue elsewhere can need sign-off here. Suspension changes and some exhaust modifications may require Low Volume Vehicle Technical Association (LVVTA) certification to legally pass a WOF. It's a straightforward process when planned for upfront, and a frustrating one when it's an afterthought after the work is already done — we'll tell you what a specific modification needs before you commit to it, not after.
